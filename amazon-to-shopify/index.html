Amazon to Shopify. A store built to convert, not just to exist.

On Amazon the traffic and the trust came with the platform. On your own store both are yours to build, and most migrations move the catalogue across without moving the thing that was doing the selling.

I build the store to convert from the first day.

Five places a store loses the order. A new store can be built to not have four of them.

  1. What’s in it

    The catalogue, brought over properly.

    Variants, images, inventory, and the product data structured so it’s readable by Shopify and by everything downstream of it.

  2. What’s in it

    Product pages built to answer a cold visitor.

    On Amazon the format did this for you. Here it’s yours: what it is, who it’s for, what it costs delivered, what happens if it’s wrong.

  3. What’s in it

    The path to checkout designed once rather than fixed later.

    Cart, shipping presentation, express checkout placement.

  4. What’s in it

    Product schema from the start,

    so Merchant Center and search read the catalogue correctly — cheaper to do now than to retrofit.

  5. What’s in it

    Speed and mobile as build constraints,

    not a later project.

When this is the right page.

  • We’re doing well on Amazon and we don’t own the customer.

    The usual reason, and the right one.

  • We have a Shopify store we never finished.

    Common. Often the faster path — there’s something to work with.

  • We’re keeping Amazon and adding Shopify.

    Most people do, and it’s worth saying: this isn’t a migration away, it’s a second channel you control.

How it goes.

  1. Step 1

    I look at what’s selling on Amazon and why. Your listings, your reviews, the questions buyers keep asking — that’s the raw material for the product pages, and it already exists.

  2. Step 2

    I build it. Same person — the decisions about how the pages should work are made by the person building them.

  3. Step 3

    I set a baseline before launch and measure after, so there’s a number to compare against rather than a feeling.

What I won’t be doing.

No ads, no Amazon account management, no fulfilment or logistics setup, no off-site SEO. And no promise about traffic — a new store starts with none, and nothing on this page says otherwise.

What I build is the store. Getting people to it is a different conversation.
