  3. Landing pages for paid traffic

    03 / 08

    When the ad works and the page doesn’t.

    Your click-through is fine. The page the click lands on is where it falls apart — a promise made in the ad and not met in the first screen, a form too early, a load time that eats the visit.

    • The first screen rebuilt against the ad.

      Whatever the creative promises — the offer, the product, the claim — is what the page opens with. Most mismatches are here.

    • One action, above the fold, on a phone.

      Not three competing buttons and a newsletter signup.

    • The objection order fixed.

      Paid traffic is colder than organic, so the questions arrive in a different sequence.

    Landing pages for paid traffic→

  4. Amazon to Shopify

    04 / 08

    A store built to convert, not just to exist.

    On Amazon the traffic and the trust came with the platform. On your own store both are yours to build, and most migrations move the catalogue across without moving the thing that was doing the selling.

    • The catalogue, brought over properly.

      Variants, images, inventory, and the product data structured so it’s readable by Shopify and by everything downstream of it.

    • Product pages built to answer a cold visitor.

      On Amazon the format did this for you. Here it’s yours: what it is, who it’s for, what it costs delivered.

    • The path to checkout designed once rather than fixed later.

      Cart, shipping presentation, express checkout placement.

    Amazon to Shopify→

  5. Product schema & Merchant Center

    05 / 08

    Fix Shopify product schema and Merchant Center errors.

    Products disapproved, rich results not showing, structured data that doesn’t validate — usually because the theme emits it in a way the spec doesn’t accept, or an app and the theme are both emitting it.

    • Product schema that validates.

      The required and recommended properties, emitted once rather than twice, matching what’s actually on the page.

    • The duplicate-emission problem found and resolved.

      A theme and a review app both emitting Product is one of the most common causes of a feed that won’t behave.

    • Merchant Center feed errors traced to their source.

      Whether that’s the product data, the schema, or a policy mismatch.

    Product schema & Merchant Center→

  6. AI shopping visibility

    06 / 08

    What’s already automatic, and what actually decides whether you’re recommended.

    Your products are probably already in ChatGPT and Google’s AI surfaces. Being listed isn’t the same as being understood — and understood is what decides whether you get recommended.

    • Attribute completeness, product by product.

      Brand, category, product type, materials, size, variants, identifiers, availability. The boring fields.

    • “Who this is for”, written into the product data.

      AI shopping questions arrive as constraints — a budget, a use case, a restriction. A product that doesn’t state its own use case can’t be matched to one.

    • Comparison material.

      What this product is better at, what it isn’t, what it doesn’t do. Stated plainly enough to be quoted.

    AI shopping visibility→

Why you need me?

Traffic that arrives and doesn’t buy is almost always losing in one of three places.

  1. 01

    01

    The product page.

    The visitor can't tell in the first few seconds what the product is, who it's for, or why this one. They scroll, they don't find it, they leave. This is the most common one by a long way.

  2. 02

    02

    The path to checkout.

    Add-to-cart works, then something between the cart and the payment screen costs you the order — a form field, a shipping surprise, a step that breaks on mobile.

  3. 03

    03

    The mobile experience specifically.

    Most of your traffic is on a phone. If the price sits below the fold, if the gallery doesn't swipe, if the buttons are too close together — desktop looks fine and the revenue isn't there.

I don’t guess which one it is. I look at your funnel data next to your actual pages, and the data says which.
