Why your Shopify store gets traffic but no sales (and how to find the leak)

If your store has visitors but few orders, the problem almost never is the traffic — it's a conversion leak you can't see. Here's how to find it, name it, and fix the one that's worth the most first.

If your Shopify store gets traffic but few sales, the problem is almost never the traffic — it’s a conversion leak, usually on the product page or in the checkout. The five most common leaks are: a buried or missing trust signal, a slow-loading product page, shipping cost revealed too late, a weak or generic add-to-cart section, and a checkout that asks for too much too soon. Fixing the single highest-impact one first typically recovers more revenue than doubling your ad spend.

That’s the short answer. The rest of this article is how you actually find which leak is costing you the most — because the fix that transforms one store barely moves another, and guessing is how brands burn a quarter optimising the wrong thing.

First, the reframe that changes everything

Most founders treat conversion rate as a fixed property of their store — a number they were dealt. It isn’t. It’s the most leveraged number in your entire business. Here’s the arithmetic that proves it.

Take a store buying 10,000 visitors a month at a $60 average order value. Hold the traffic and the ad spend exactly the same, and look at what conversion rate alone does to the revenue:

Conversion rateMonthly ordersMonthly revenue
0.5%50$3,000
1.5%150$9,000
2.5%250$15,000

5×the revenue from 0.5% → 2.5% — same visitors, same ad budget, not one new ad run.

The leverage was already sitting inside your funnel, unclaimed. This is why “we need more traffic” is usually the wrong instinct: more traffic poured into a leaking store just leaks faster — and at $3–8 a click, that maths goes underwater quickly. Seal the leak first, and every visitor after it, paid or organic, is worth more.

That’s the whole game. Now let’s find your leak.

The five leaks — and the signal that reveals each

A leak isn’t a vibe. Each one leaves a fingerprint in your data or on the page itself. Here’s the map — then the detail on each, including exactly where to look to confirm it’s yours.

Leak 1 — A buried or missing trust signal

The buyer reaches the moment of decision — the add-to-cart button — and there’s nothing there to reassure them. No guarantee, no review count, no return policy in sight. Reviews might exist, but they’re 2,000 pixels down the page where most buyers never scroll. The visitor hesitates, tells themselves “I’ll come back later,” and never does.

Signal: In a session recording or heatmap, watch how far buyers scroll. If engagement falls off a cliff before your social proof, they’re deciding without it. A high product-page-to-cart drop-off on a well-trafficked page is the tell.

Why it leaks: Trust is consumed at the point of hesitation, not at the bottom of the page. A review a buyer never sees does zero work.

Leak 2 — A slow-loading product page

Your product page takes four, five, six seconds to become usable — especially on mobile, on a mid-range phone, on real-world 4G. Every one of those seconds sheds buyers who will never tell you they left. Bloated apps, uncompressed hero images, and render-blocking scripts are the usual culprits.

Signal: Run the page through PageSpeed Insights and read the field data (real Chrome users), not just the lab score — watch Largest Contentful Paint and Interaction to Next Paint. In GA4, compare conversion by device: if mobile converts at a fraction of desktop on similar traffic, speed is a prime suspect.

Why it leaks: Speed is a conversion input, not a technical nicety. A buyer who bounces before the page renders never entered your funnel at all. (Fixing the foundations that cause it — crawl, render, and Core Web Vitals — is the job of Shopify technical SEO.)

Leak 3 — Shipping cost revealed too late

The buyer moves through the whole flow believing one price, then gets ambushed by a shipping charge on the final step. By then they’ve committed emotionally, they see an unexpected total, and they feel tricked — so they abandon. This is the single most reliable way to lose a buyer who was ready to purchase.

Signal: Your checkout abandonment rate is the headline number. Look specifically at the drop between the shipping step and payment. If a large share of carts die exactly where the shipping cost appears, you’ve found it.

Why it leaks: Surprise at the last step reads as dishonesty, even when it isn’t. The cost isn’t the problem — the timing of the cost is.

Leak 4 — A weak or generic add-to-cart section

A product name, a price, and an “Add to cart” button — and nothing else doing any persuading. No answer to “why this one?”, no handling of the obvious objection, no urgency that’s actually true. The buy box is where the sale is won or lost, and on most stores it’s the most neglected part of the page.

Signal: Look at your add-to-cart rate. Then read your own buy box as a skeptical first-time visitor: does anything here answer why buy this, why now, why from you? If it’s just a spec and a button, it’s leaking.

Why it leaks: The buy box is the decision point. Generic there means you’re relying on the buyer to persuade themselves. (Rebuilding the buy box to actually sell is the core of Shopify product page development.)

Leak 5 — A checkout that asks for too much, too soon

Forced account creation before purchase. A dozen form fields. A phone number demanded for a t-shirt. Every extra field and every unnecessary step is another chance for a ready buyer to reconsider and leave.

Signal: Checkout funnel visualisation in GA4 or your checkout analytics. Watch completion step by step; the field or step where it collapses is your friction point. Forced account creation is a classic single-step killer.

Why it leaks: At checkout the buyer has already decided yes. Every additional demand is a fresh chance to change their mind — for no gain to you.

The principle that decides what to fix first

Here’s where most stores go wrong even after they’ve found their leaks: they fix the easiest one, not the most valuable one. Reordering the buy box is quick, so it gets done first — while the checkout haemorrhaging a third of ready buyers sits untouched because it feels harder.

Prioritise by revenue impact, not by ease. Estimate what each leak is costing you, then fix the biggest number first — even if it’s the hardest. A rough model beats a gut feeling every time:

monthly visitors × the conversion lift you’d expect × average order value = what the fix is worth

Run that for each leak. The biggest product wins, regardless of how easy the others look. Effort is a tiebreaker between fixes of similar value — never the deciding factor. (This is the same discipline behind the five CRO mistakes that quietly cost DTC brands revenue — fixing by test and impact, not by opinion.)

A worked example

Take an illustrative store — 10,000 visitors a month, $60 AOV, converting at 1.2%. That’s 120 orders, $7,200 a month.

Session recordings show buyers reaching the buy box and stalling: Leak 4. It’s a bare spec and a button, reviews stranded far below. So the fix is scoped narrowly — pull three real trust signals up to the add-to-cart, rewrite the section to answer why this product, and handle the one objection that keeps recurring.

Say that lifts conversion from 1.2% to 1.8% — a realistic move for a buy box that was doing no persuading at all:

+$3,600a month — 180 orders instead of 120 from the same 10,000 visitors. One page, one focused change, zero extra ad spend.

That’s the shape of real CRO. Not a redesign. Not more traffic. One leak, found and named, then closed — and it compounds every month it stays fixed.

The 5-signal self-check you can run today

Before you touch anything, spend twenty minutes gathering these five numbers. They tell you where your leak lives without any guesswork:

  1. Product-page-to-cart rate — of everyone who views a product, what share adds to cart? Low here points at Leaks 1 and 4 (trust and the buy box).
  2. Cart-to-checkout rate — of carts, what share begins checkout? A gap here often means shipping surprise (Leak 3).
  3. Checkout completion, step by step — where exactly do ready buyers drop? That step is your Leak 5.
  4. Mobile vs. desktop conversion — a wide gap on similar traffic points at speed (Leak 2), or a mobile buy box below the fold.
  5. PageSpeed field data on your top product page — real-user LCP and INP, not the lab score.

Write down where the biggest drop is. That’s your leak. Run the revenue model on fixing it. That’s your priority.

The bottom line

A store with traffic and no sales doesn’t need more traffic — it needs the one leak that’s quietly costing the most, found, named, and closed. The five above cover the overwhelming majority of what I see on high-traffic Shopify stores, and the self-check will point you at yours today.
