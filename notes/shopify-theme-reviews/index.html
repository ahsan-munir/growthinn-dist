I read 1,471 Shopify theme reviews. Here's what they say.

Every review on five Shopify themes — 1,471 of them, collected and counted.

The short version

I collected every Theme Store review for five themes — Shopify’s free Dawn and four paid themes — and read them. That is 1,471 reviews, the oldest from 2013. The paid themes are overwhelmingly well reviewed and Dawn is not, so the complaints below come from very different places. What surprised me was the shape of the unhappy ones: almost none said “bad theme”. They named a setting that wasn’t there — spacing, padding, a footer section, an image size — usually with the price next to it. Reviews are self-selected, so nothing here says what a theme is like. It says what these reviewers wrote.

That is the short version. The rest is each pattern in the reviewers’ own words, the one place the happy and unhappy reviews contradict each other, and — the part this kind of article usually skips — what 1,471 reviews cannot tell you.

How these were collected

This changes how you should read everything below, so it goes first.

  • Five themes: Shopify’s free Dawn, and four paid themes.
  • Every review on each theme’s page, collected in September 2026. That is 1,471 reviews, the oldest from 2013.
  • Positive, neutral and negative come from the Theme Store’s own icons, and I checked each theme’s totals against its page.
  • A pattern only appears below if at least three reviews say it in their own words. Each one carries its dates.
  • No reviewer is named, and I am not naming the paid themes next to criticism. This is not about any one developer.

Here is what the five look like, by the pages’ own figures:

Reviews Positive Neutral Negative
Dawn (free) 288 101 71 116
Four paid themes, together 1,183 1,134 14 35

Two very different pictures. Keep that in mind throughout: for the paid themes, every complaint below comes from a group of 49 unhappy reviews out of 1,183.

What this sample is not

Reviews are self-selected. People who are very happy or very annoyed write them; most people write nothing. That is why you will see “7 of 49 unhappy reviews” throughout and never “15% of merchants” — I do not know what the silent majority experienced.

1. The price, next to one specific missing thing (7 reviews, 2019–2026)

When a paid-theme reviewer was unhappy, they often put the price directly beside the exact thing they could not find.

“$400 and u can barely customize anything… cant space anything out, can’t make the images bigger than the set size.”

“A $340 USD theme doesn’t have a liquid section in the footer.”

“At this price theme should have provided more settings to adjust controls, spacing; more collection styles.”

“The paddings are all off. … For $400 VERY disappointed.”

It is rarely “bad theme”. It is spacing, padding, a footer section, image size, section styles. Seven of the 49 unhappy paid-theme reviews read like this.

2. Image size or crop you cannot change (4 reviews, 2023–2025)

“adjusting the photo sizes, which were far too large on a single listing page. It was a simple task, but I couldn’t do it myself because the code was restricted and messy.”

“Product photos are huge on desktop, there are awkward spaces between most blocks.”

“Crops images weird.”

3. Blurry images, whatever size you upload (4 reviews, 2017–2024)

Three of these are about Dawn’s image banner.

“no matter what size you use for the image banner, it will ALWAYS be blurry.”

“I have just updated from DAWN 2.5.0 to 6.0.2 and I am shocked that Image Banners are completely Blurry!”

The frustration in all four is the same: re-uploading at a different size did not help. Only one of these is from 2024 or later, so this may well be old news. I would still check it.

4. Asking for a change, and being sent somewhere else (6 reviews, 2016–2025)

“even for simple things they will suggest you just go to pay some other company a couple hundred bucks just to fix it”

“There is no paid customization support option from the company. They tell customers to go elsewhere.”

“They told me I would have to higher their team at $120 an hour”

Most of these are from before 2024. They are worth reading next to the next section.

5. The five-star reviews are often about the support (6 quoted, 61 found)

This is the part that surprised me. Searching the 1,134 positive paid-theme reviews, 61 mention a problem and a fix together.

“I got stuck on serious cart issue these guys solved it very efficiently in one day.”

“I submitted an issue regarding the inventory count display, and the team resolved it quickly and efficiently.”

“Every issue has been resolved in timely manner and with ease.”

61 is a keyword search across the positive reviews, not a hand count like the numbered patterns above. Treat it as “at least this many” rather than a precise figure.

Put this next to section 4 and a line runs through both. The happy reviewers quoted here describe bugs, and support fixed them. The unhappy ones in section 4 describe changes they wanted, where support said no or pointed them at a paid developer. One 2025 review says exactly that: no paid customisation option, go elsewhere.

The same support team can be genuinely excellent at bugs and genuinely unhelpful at changes, and both sets of reviewers are telling the truth. Before buying, it is worth knowing which of the two you are about to need.

6. Updates undoing work (4 reviews, four different themes)

“It updates constantly and every time it does something gets screwed up.”

“has to be manually updated (copy and paste every page section) every month or so when a new update is available”

“Recent versions even cause browser lag.”

Four reviews out of 1,471 is not many, and I nearly cut this section for that reason. It stays because the four come from four different themes, free and paid — which is a weaker signal than a count but not nothing.

7. Dawn: even the happy reviewers list what’s missing (5 reviews, 2022–2026)

“This theme is way fast… downside its basic so I have to code the sections from scratch.” (positive)

“It is missing some basic features like a pop up feature, a back to top button and some more sections would be nice. But it’s very stable, smooth design.” (positive)

“missing so many basic features like sidebars. And no support community on how to get them installed or how to code them back in.” (negative)

Two of the five are positive reviews. People like Dawn and still write down what they had to add — which is arguably the most useful kind of review there is.

If you’re choosing a theme

Every item here comes from something above. I cannot tell you these checks lead to a better result; I can tell you they are what these reviewers found out after buying.

Before you buy

  • Spacing and padding: can you change them between sections in the editor, without code?
  • Footer: is there a custom Liquid or HTML section you can put there?
  • Product images: can you set their size and crop?
  • Image banner: put one of your own photos in it and look at it on a phone.
  • Changes: ask the developer whether they do small customisations, and if not, what they suggest instead.
  • Updates: ask how they work, and whether your changes survive them.
  • On Dawn: list what you need that isn't there, and decide who will build it.

If you build themes, or build on them

The desires in these reviews are unusually concrete. People asked for different hero images for mobile and desktop; size as a dropdown and colour as swatches by default; control over whether filters start open or closed; footer links and images without editing HTML; image zoom; more typography control.

That list comes from reviewers’ own “I wish it had” sentences rather than from the counted patterns above, so it carries no numbers — it is what people asked for, not how many asked.

And several of the unhappy reviews above are not about bugs at all. They are about the gap between the settings a theme ships with and the change the merchant wanted to make. That gap is a build decision someone made long before the merchant arrived.

What 1,471 reviews can’t tell you

Who didn’t write a review. Whether the problems were fixed later. Whether a complaint from 2016 still applies to the theme sold today. And whether the reviewer’s store was set up in a way that caused the problem in the first place. A review is one person’s account, and I have treated it as exactly that.

What’s next

I am adding more themes. If a pattern above stops holding up as the set grows, I will say so here.
